For fine fractions, the particles should be fine enough that X-rays and ICP (Inductively Coupled Plasma) can detect them. This means the particles must be small enough to scatter X-rays and interact with ICP, typically less than 1 micron in diameter.

The Bragg equation is given by \(n\lambda = 2d\sin\theta\), where \(\lambda\) is the wavelength of the X-rays (1.54 nm), \(n\) is an integer (usually 1 for the first-order reflection), and \(\theta\) is the angle of incidence. Given the interatomic spacing \(a = 0.28\) nm, we can solve for \(\theta\) and then \(\sin^2\theta\):

\[
\sin\theta = \frac{\lambda}{2d} = \frac{1.54}{2 \times 0.28} \approx 2.75 \text{ nm}
\]

\[
\theta = \sin^{-1}(2.75) \approx 156.87^\circ
\]

Since \(\sin^2\theta = \sin^2(156.87^\circ) \approx 0.0006\), we can now calculate the d-spacing for the crystal:

\[
d = \frac{n\lambda}{2\sin\theta} = \frac{1.54}{2 \times 2.75} \approx 0.28 \text{ nm}
\]
